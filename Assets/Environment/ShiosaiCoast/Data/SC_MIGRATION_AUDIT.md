### Facts the spec does not state, found in the repo (must be handled by the migration)

1. **The project is in Gamma color space** (`m_ActiveColorSpace: 0`). HDRP requires **Linear**.
   This is a global, project-wide visual change that will alter *every* existing region
   (Sakura Pass, Maple City, Azora Highlands), not just Shiosai. It is the single largest
   blast-radius item in the migration and is why the checkpoint exists.
2. **Windows graphics APIs are on "auto"** (`Direct3D12, Direct3D11`). Spec §4.2.6 wants a
   deliberate order — currently it is merely the default that happens to start at DX12.
3. **Quality settings have six levels** (`Very Low … Ultra`), not the four Windows presets the
   spec asks for (§4.2.4). The mapping from 6 existing levels → Low/Medium/High/Ultra HDRP
   assets is an open decision.
4. **Two additional Built-in shaders** not in the §4.4 matrix are in live use and will go pink:
   `MapleRide/SakuraRoad` (the asphalt the rider actually looks at) and
   `Hidden/MapleRide/SakuraPostFX` (the image-effect grade, which HDRP Volumes replace).
   `MapleRide/SakuraFoliage`, `MapleRide/SakuraSky`, `MapleRide/MapleCityFacade` and
   `MapleRide/AttenDebug` are in the same position.
5. **Shiosai has no dedicated scene.** It is generated into the 200 MB `SakuraPass.unity`
   alongside every other region, which is exactly what spec §6.1 forbids for production art.
6. **`ShiosaiCoastDiagnostics.Capture` writes the scene file** after restoring the region
   (scene timestamp changes on every capture). Harmless today, but it means capture runs are
   not read-only; the HDRP capture tool should be.

### The bug that made the first three conversion runs lie

`ConvertMaterials` reported `gltf_remapped=125` on **every** run with an identical count.
A conversion that never decreases is not converting. Two causes, both found by probe:

1. `AssetDatabase.FindAssets("t:Model")` matched **nothing**. It only covers Unity's
   `ModelImporter`; this project's riders, bikes, props and road models are `.glb` handled
   by a glTF **ScriptedImporter**. Fixed by scanning `*.glb/*.gltf/*.fbx` by extension.
2. `AssetDatabase.ExtractAsset` is not implemented by that ScriptedImporter and returned an
   error that was only logged as a warning. Fixed by the general mechanism instead:
   author a real `.mat` and `importer.AddRemap(...)` to it, which survives reimport.

Result: **313 embedded materials extracted from 125 models** into
`Assets/Materials/Extracted/`, and the swap now persists.

### Colour-space bridge (new, deliberate)

`MapleRideHDRPCommon.hlsl` gained `MR_Authored()` / `MR_ToRender()`. The kit's cel maths,
light colours and ambient bands were authored against a **Gamma** project. Linear changes
both ends: sRGB textures are linearised on sample, and shader output is sRGB-encoded for
display. The bridge keeps the cel maths in its authored space and converts only at the
boundaries. `MR_ToRender` **clamps before converting**, because the Gamma pipeline clamped at
display - without that, a cel highlight of 1.4 was amplified by the 2.4-power curve and blew
the road, guardrails and tree crowns to flat white.

### Tooling trap worth keeping

Unity does **not** reliably recompile a `.shader` when only an `.hlsl` it includes changed.
Two consecutive captures came back pixel-identical after a real fix. `MapleRideShaderReimport`
(`MapleRide/Environment/Reimport HDRP Shaders`) now forces it and must be run between editing
the include and capturing.

### Deliberate deviations from the section 4.4 matrix

- `ShiosaiArchitecture` and the glTF materials route to **CelLit**, not `HDRP/Lit`. HDRP/Lit
  is physically lit in lux while this scene's lights are authored at intensity ~1 and HDRP's
  static-sky ambient is off, so HDRP/Lit surfaces rendered black.
- HDRP's stock `Bloom`, `ScreenSpaceAmbientOcclusion` and filmic `Tonemapping` are disabled:
  none existed in the Built-in baseline, and the bloom blew the ocean sun-glint into a white
  disc that swallowed the harbour. To be re-introduced deliberately as art direction.
- Cast shadows remain **PENDING** for hand-written passes (no HDRP light-loop data).

### C4 - terrain and sky drew nothing at all (the largest defect, found last)

Symptom: the harbour-road capture came back with no ground and no horizon - a single flat navy
field above and below, with the road, trees and houses floating in it.

Every instrument said the scene was healthy, and each one was telling the truth about the wrong
thing:

* all six hand-written HDRP shaders reported `supported=True`, `errors=0`;
* the material census reported every Shiosai material correctly on an HDRP shader;
* the §23 gate reported zero pink / missing-shader materials.

Root cause: the fog fix for C2 added `positionRWS` to the shared `MRVaryings` struct, and
`MR_Vert` in `MapleRideHDRPCommon.hlsl` was updated to write it. But **three shaders carry their
own vertex function** - `MR_VertTerrain`, `MR_VertFoliage`, `MR_VertSky` - and none of them wrote
the new member. `MR_ApplyFog` therefore read an uninitialised interpolator, and the resulting
garbage fog distance discarded every terrain pixel.

Note the failure mode: this is not a compile error, so no shader-health check can see it, and
under HDRP an absent surface looks identical to an absent *object*. Fixed by writing
`o.positionRWS = positionRWS` in all three custom vertex functions.

**Lesson to carry forward:** adding a member to a shared varyings struct is a breaking change to
every custom vertex function that uses it. There is no compiler diagnostic for it.

### C5 - cross-material texture leak (the SRP Batcher)

Symptom, visible only once C4 was fixed and the terrain started drawing again: the carriageway
rendered covered in the terrain's grass albedo, and the terrain rendered covered in bark.

Ruled out, in order, by direct measurement rather than inference:

1. *Wrong texture assigned?* No - `Shiosai_Asphalt._MainTex = Shiosai_Asphalt_Albedo`, and that
   PNG is correct asphalt when opened.
2. *A global texture override?* No - the project calls `SetGlobalTexture` nowhere.
3. *Foliage geometry lying on the road?* No - a spatial overlap probe found only the carriageway,
   its markings and the terrain shell at the road surface; no foliage.
4. *Terrain drawing on top of the road?* No - the road silhouette, its crown and its lane-marking
   strips were all still crisply visible, just wrongly textured.

The decisive clue: `Shiosai_LinePaint` has **no** `_MainTex` assigned at all, and it was grass-
textured too - so the texture slot was inheriting whatever the *previous draw* had left bound.
Confirmed by isolation: disabling the SRP Batcher fixes it completely, in one capture.

Unity's own verdict (via `ShaderUtil.GetSRPBatcherCompatibilityCode`, reached by reflection)
is that **all six** hand-written MapleRide HDRP shaders are `NOT COMPATIBLE` (code 1), even
though a static audit of their `Properties` blocks against their `UnityPerMaterial` CBUFFERs
finds no mismatch in any of them.

**Decision: ship with `enableSRPBatcher = 0` on all four MapleRide HDRP quality tiers.**
Rationale: the batcher was never batching these six shaders anyway (it rejects all of them), so
the setting costs us nothing on MapleRide surfaces - it only forgoes batching on stock `HDRP/Lit`
and glTF materials, and it buys demonstrably correct rendering. Correctness over an unmeasured
performance gain. Reversible via `MapleRide/Environment/HDRP Batcher - Enable`.

**Follow-up (not this milestone):** make the six shaders genuinely SRP-Batcher-compatible and
re-enable. Until then this is a catalogued, deliberate deviation, not an oversight.

### C3 - harbour huts rendered pure white  *(fixed, previously reported)*

Seven materials were stranded on `HDRP/Lit`, which is lit in physical lux while this project's
lights are authored at intensity ~1-3. `ConvertMaterials` kept its own inline branches, so the
`HDRP/Lit -> CelLit` recovery only ever ran over *scene* materials. Fixed by routing all three
conversion entry points through one shared `ConvertOne()`.

### C6 - the sky was a flat navy slab

Root cause: the project's sky is a Built-in **skybox material** on `RenderSettings.skybox`, and
**HDRP ignores `RenderSettings.skybox` entirely** - it draws only the sky selected by the
`VisualEnvironment` volume override, which the earlier white-out fix had set to `None`.

Fixed by enabling an HDRP **Gradient Sky**, with its three bands sampled from the pre-HDRP
baseline capture so the HDRP sky lands on the colour the region was art-directed against:

| Band   | Sampled sRGB            | Applied as |
|--------|-------------------------|------------|
| top    | 0.557, 0.604, 0.686     | `.linear`  |
| middle | 0.660, 0.690, 0.730     | `.linear`  |
| bottom | 0.800, 0.800, 0.780     | `.linear`  |

Gradient Sky was chosen over `PhysicallyBasedSky` (which previously blew the scene out) and
`HDRISky` (no authored HDRI exists). **PROVISIONAL** - matched to the Built-in baseline, not to
the SC01-SC08 reference set; the art-directed sky/atmosphere pass is spec §8-16.

### Latent bug found and fixed while diagnosing C4

`ShiosaiCoastEnvironment.LoadOrCreate` re-assigned `mat.shader = Shader.Find(<Built-in name>)`
on **every** build. Any asset-level HDRP conversion was therefore silently reverted the next time
the coast was regenerated. This had not yet bitten the captures, but it would have turned the
whole conversion back to Built-in on the next environment build.

Fixed by introducing `Assets/Environment/Shared/Scripts/MapleRideShaderNames.cs`, a single
resolver mapping surface role -> active shader. It returns the HDRP replacement only when one
exists **and** compiles, and falls through to the Built-in name when no SRP is active - so it
also satisfies the §4.1 rule that no Built-in shader is deleted, and a rollback to the checkpoint
keeps working with no code change. Shiosai's generator is wired through it.

**Not yet wired (out of Shiosai scope, deliberate):** the Sakura Pass, Maple City, Azora, Taka and
Fuji generators still hard-code Built-in shader names in their own `LoadOrCreate`. They carry the
same latent revert. Route to fix-implementer.

## 8. Milestone C closeout - D1 and D5 resolved

### D1 - distant trees rendered black. ROOT CAUSE WAS NOT FOG.

The working theory in section 7 (under-strength aerial haze) was wrong. The fog term was
binding correctly and at the right density; Shiosai's ambience is
`fog = (0.60, 0.77, 0.90)`, `density = 0.00026`, ExponentialSquared, set by
`RegionDirector.ApplyAmbience`. The "warm cream 0.00055" recorded earlier in this document
was **Maple City's** ambience, misattributed.

The actual cause is a colour-space mismatch on material `Color` properties:

- The project is now **Linear** (section 4.2.7). Unity uploads a material `Color` property to
  the GPU **already linearised**.
- The MapleRide cel shaders do all of their maths in **authored sRGB** space, then convert once
  at the end via `MR_ToRender`. Texture samples were already bridged with `MR_Authored`;
  `Color` properties were not.
- The error is nearly invisible on bright tints and catastrophic on dark ones:

  | authored value | uploaded (linear) | error |
  |---|---|---|
  | 0.92 | 0.83 | -10% (imperceptible) |
  | 0.19 | 0.030 | **6.3x too dark** |

  `Shiosai_DistantTrees` is authored (0.10, 0.19, 0.15) - squarely in the catastrophic band,
  which is exactly why it alone read as black.

**Fix:** added `MR_AuthoredCol()` (float3/float4) to `MapleRideHDRPCommon.hlsl` and applied it
to every consumed `Color` property across all six hand-written HDRP shaders - 31 usage sites:

| Shader | Properties bridged |
|---|---|
| `MapleRideCelLit.shader` | `_Color`, `_HeightTint`, `_SnowColor`, `_ShadeColor`, `_SpecTint`, `_RimColor`, `_MossColor`, `_WearColor` |
| `MapleRideTerrain.shader` | `_GrassColor`, `_RockColor`, `_ScreeColor`, `_SoilColor`, `_MossColor`, `_PetalColor`, `_SnowColor`, `_ShadeColor`, `_SpecTint`, `_RimColor` |
| `MapleRideFoliage.shader` | `_Color`, `_ShadeColor`, `_TransColor`, `_RimColor` |
| `MapleRideOcean.shader` | `_ShallowColor`, `_DeepColor`, `_SkyTint`, `_SunColor`, `_FoamColor` |
| `MapleRideShallows.shader` | `_ShoreColor`, `_ShelfColor`, `_FoamColor` |
| `MapleRideSky.shader` | `_ZenithColor`, `_MidColor`, `_HorizonColor`, `_GroundColor`, `_SunColor`, `_CloudColor` |

**Verified by render:** `good_graphics/diag_shiosai_harbour_road.png` and
`diag_shiosai_harbour.png`. Distant conifers read dark green, mid-ground grass reads warm
green, and the mountain range layers away in pale blue. Compared side by side against
`SC/01-*.png`, `SC/07-*.png`, `SC/08-*.png`: the depth layering now reads correctly, and in
fact closer to the references than the pre-HDRP baseline, whose flat grey fog crushed the
whole background into one plane. No haze-density change was needed or made.

### D5 - ocean rendered milky-pale from one angle and pure black from another. NEW, found during D1 verification.

`VertOcean` in `MapleRideOcean.shader` is a custom vertex function and **never wrote
`o.positionRWS`**. `MR_ApplyFog` consumes that interpolator, so the fog distance was garbage,
and the sign of the garbage decided whether the sea washed out to fog colour or collapsed to
black.

This is the **third** instance of the same bug class in this migration (after
`MR_VertTerrain` / `MR_VertFoliage` / `MR_VertSky` in section 7, defect C4).

> **Invariant:** every custom vertex function returning `MRVaryings` MUST write **every**
> member, `positionRWS` above all. An unwritten interpolator produces **no compiler
> diagnostic** - the only symptom is a wrong or absent surface. A full audit of all six
> shaders plus the shared `MR_Vert` was run after this fix; `VertOcean` was the last offender.

**Verified by render:** `good_graphics/diag_shiosai_sea_torii.png` - the sea is now opaque
deep blue with a turquoise shallow shelf and clean cel banding at the shoreline, matching the
structure of `pre_hdrp_baseline/diag_shiosai_sea_torii.png` and the SC reference set.
`diag_shiosai_harbour_road.png` confirms the cove no longer renders black.

### How it was derived, and why not authored from scratch

`ShiosaiSceneBuilder.Run` opens `SakuraPass.unity`, prunes the art and crowd roots owned by
other regions **in memory**, and writes the result to a NEW path. `SakuraPass.unity` is never
saved over - verified: its mtime is unchanged by the split, so the pre-migration state and the
other regions' scene are byte-identical (spec 4.1).

Deriving rather than authoring is deliberate. The ride rig - `RideBootstrap`, `RegionDirector`,
`RideSession`, `DeviceManager`, `RouteDirector`, the HUDs, the Kuro rider with `KuroRoadGrounding`
/ `RouteFollower` / `KuroBikeRig`, the follow camera, and the key/fill lights that the ambience
system drives **by exact name** - is validated, hand-tuned state that is not reproducible by
script. Deriving preserves every one of those gameplay contracts exactly.

Roots pruned (exact-name match, all matches removed): `Sakura Pass Environment` (x3),
`Azora Highlands Environment`, `Maple City Environment`, `Taka Mountains Environment`,
`Fuji Ridge Environment`, `Azora NPCs`, `Maple City NPCs`, `Taka NPCs`, `NPCs`.
11 roots, 86,354 renderers removed.

### RegionDirector change

`RegionDirector.ApplyEnvironmentVisibility` resolved region roots from the **active scene only**.
With Shiosai in its own scene - and additive segment scenes coming per spec 6.2 - that would
silently fail to find, and therefore fail to show or hide, a region root. `FindRoots` now scans
the roots of **every loaded scene**. Behaviour in the single-scene case is unchanged.

`ShiosaiCoastEnvironment.ScenePath` became a settable field (default unchanged) so the coast can
be built into its own scene; `ShiosaiCoastDiagnostics` and `ShiosaiMigrationAudit` now prefer
`SC_Persistent.unity` and fall back to `SakuraPass.unity` if the split has not been run.
